Expanding the square of a sum produces three terms: the first term squared, twice the product of both terms, and the second term squared. This fundamental law holds true for all integers, fractions, and real exponent values.

Euler's Identity connects the five most fundamental mathematical constants in a single elegant equation: ee (the base of natural logarithms), ii (the imaginary unit), π\pi (the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter), 1 (the multiplicative identity), and 0 (the additive identity). It is a special case of Euler's formula eiθ=cosθ+isinθe^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta evaluated at θ=π\theta = \pi.
